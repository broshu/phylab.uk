Cyclotron: how it works

① Structure and roles

Two semicircular D-shaped metal boxes sit in a vacuum, with a gap between them connected to a high-frequency alternating supply; the magnetic field points out of the page, perpendicular to the boxes.

The gap electric field accelerates the particle and the magnetic field in the boxes turns its path. The metal boxes shield the inside from electric fields, and the Lorentz force does no work, so all the kinetic energy comes from the electric field.

② Orbital period (the key condition)

Inside the boxes the Lorentz force provides the centripetal force:

The period does not depend on speed or radius, so every half turn always takes T/2. This is what makes repeated, synchronised acceleration possible.

③ Synchronisation with the supply

The period T0 of the alternating voltage must equal the orbital period:

Each time the particle reaches the gap the field has just reversed, so it is accelerated once every half turn, twice per full turn. One machine (with a fixed frequency) can accelerate only particles with the same charge-to-mass ratio; deuterons and α particles have the same ratio, so they can share it.

Key conclusions

① The maximum kinetic energy does not depend on the accelerating voltage U; it is set only by B, R and the charge-to-mass ratio. To raise the final energy you must increase B or R.

② Increasing U leaves the final energy unchanged, but the number of accelerations N and the time t in the machine both decrease.

③ The magnetic field only bends the path and never accelerates; the electric field accelerates once every half turn.

④ The period and frequency of the alternating supply are set by the particle’s orbital period.

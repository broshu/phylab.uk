Physics model and the ideal plan ＋

1 · Thrust on the ice

Air leaves the nozzle at u = 500 m/s. The thrust is the momentum carried away per second:

F=uΔmΔt=500×40=20 kN,a=Fm=10 m/s2

Ice has no friction, so once the jet stops the car keeps its speed. The fastest straight is: burn first, then coast. Reaching speed v costs

qAB=mvu=4v(kg)

A side jet on the ice gives a sideways velocity that nothing can remove. You either slide off the ice or reach B already skidding.

2 · Holding the curve

To stay on the arc the car needs a net force toward O. Friction can supply up to μN, with N = mg + Ffan; the side jet supplies S:

mv2R=f+S,|f|≤μN

While the tyres roll this is static friction, and it takes whatever value is needed. So the side thrust must stay inside a window:

mv2R−μN≤S≤mv2R+μN

Too little and the car slides outward; too much and it is pushed in toward O. Either way the tyres slide, friction becomes kinetic (μN, against the sliding), and the car can no longer be steered round the arc.

3 · The ideal plan

Use the least side thrust, S = mv2/R − μN, for the time t = πR/(2v). The air left after the straight must last the curve:

4v+(mv2R−μN)u·πR2v=90

With the fan on and the textbook value π = 3 this gives 10v−360v=90, so v = 12 m/s: 48 kg on the straight and 42 kg in the curve, 6.85 s in total. Entering any faster leaves too little air for the curve.

The game draws a true circle (π = 3.14159…), so its ideal plan is slightly slower.

Model assumptions

  • Mass stays 2000 kg (the 90 kg of air lost is ignored); no air drag or rolling resistance; nozzle exit pressure equals the outside pressure, so F = u·Δm/Δt.
  • The car is treated as a point. The tail jet pushes along the lane; the side jet pushes straight toward O on the curve (toward the inner edge on the ice). Tyres roll freely along the lane, so friction acts only across it.
  • Skidding means the tyres slide across the lane: friction is then kinetic, μN, opposite the sliding, and the run is lost. Beyond the road edge the run-off area is treated as frictionless. This is a classroom illustration, not a prediction of how a real car spins.
  • The centripetal force is the net force toward O, not an extra force. The dashed line shows its direction; blue arrows are jet thrust, amber arrows friction, all at 1 m per 2.5 kN. The car is drawn 1.35× larger and the air jets are slowed down so they can be seen.

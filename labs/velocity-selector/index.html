Velocity Selector: how it works

Balance condition

A particle that travels in a straight line has an electric force and a Lorentz force of equal size and opposite direction:

The result does not depend on the charge, the mass or the sign of the charge. It depends only on the speed, which is why it is called a velocity selector.

What if the particle is too fast or too slow

If the speed is too small, the Lorentz force is too weak, the electric force wins, and the particle deflects in the direction of the electric force.

If the speed is too large, the Lorentz force wins and the particle deflects in the direction of the Lorentz force. After deflecting it hits a plate or the barrier and is filtered out.

The directions must match

The electric force and the Lorentz force must point in opposite directions to cancel. Set the magnetic field to “out of the page” and the two forces add in the same direction, so a particle of any speed is deflected.

Changing the sign of the charge reverses both forces at once, so they still balance: a velocity selector works for either sign of charge, but the particle cannot enter from the opposite direction.

Lengths and times in the demonstration are scaled, and the offset from the centre line is in screen pixels. “FE/q” and “FB/q” are the forces per unit charge, in N/C; when they are equal the forces on the particle balance.

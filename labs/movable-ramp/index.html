The physics

1. Horizontal momentum is conserved

mvx + MV = mv0

The ground is smooth, so there is no horizontal external force on the system. As the block climbs, it passes some of its horizontal momentum to the base.

2. At the highest point the relative velocity is zero

vx = V = mv0 / (m + M)

The highest point may be on the surface or in the air. In both cases the block is momentarily at rest relative to the base, but still moves to the right relative to the ground, so it still has horizontal kinetic energy.

3. After leaving the entrance

vx = (m − M)v0 / (m + M)

When the block leaves the entrance its vertical velocity is zero. From then on only gravity acts and it moves as a horizontal projectile; if its horizontal velocity is exactly zero it falls straight down. The run ends when the height above the ground reaches 0.

When does the block fly out?

The arc radius is R = 1.50 m and the arc length is πR/2 ≈ 2.36 m; the size is fixed. The block leaves through the top only if v0 > √[2gR(m + M)/M]. At exactly the critical value the block just reaches the top and returns. The tangent at the top is vertical, so when the block flies out it has the same horizontal velocity as the base, and it falls back into the surface.

Model assumptions: the block is a point mass with no rotation; the ground and the curved surface are both smooth, and g = 9.8 m/s². The surface is a fixed quarter circle whose tangent is horizontal at the entrance and vertical at the exit. During flight only gravity acts and the base moves at constant velocity; when the block comes back to the top it is tangent to the surface and slides on smoothly, with no collision energy loss. The entrance is 0.40 m above the ground, and after leaving the entrance the block keeps moving until it first touches the ground. The final frame shows the instant of touchdown; collision and bounce are not simulated. Gravitational potential energy is zero at the ground, y is the height above the ground, and the predicted rise h is measured from the entrance. The picture is scaled to fit the whole motion of each run, while the physical sizes do not change.

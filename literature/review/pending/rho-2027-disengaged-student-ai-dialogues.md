## Summary

研究对 246 名初中生与可教智能体的 5,522 条对话进行编码，区分六类对话脱离行为。部分行为与后测成绩呈正相关，但在只用人工标注数据的敏感性分析中未能复现；表现最好的学生群体有适度脱离并能迅速回到任务。

## Key finding A

Middle-school students often mocked, misled or ignored an AI teachable agent in maths dialogues. Most such episodes ended within a few turns, and the highest-performing students combined some disengagement with quick recovery.

> 初中生在与 AI 可教智能体的数学对话中常出现嘲弄、误导或不回应；多数此类片段几轮内结束，成绩最好的学生既有一定脱离又能迅速回到任务。

## Key finding B

Coding 5,522 utterances from 246 students into six disengagement types, the authors found False Answering and Mocking positively associated with post-test scores after controlling for pre-test, but this did not replicate on human-annotated utterances only. Withholding tended to persist.

> 作者把 246 名学生的 5,522 条发言编码为六类脱离行为。控制前测后，错误作答和嘲弄与后测成绩正相关，但仅用人工标注发言时未能复现；拒绝回应往往会持续。

## Limitations

Mathematics, not physics, with middle-school students on one platform. The positive associations did not survive the sensitivity analysis, so they should not be read as effects.

> 对象为单一平台上的初中数学而非物理；正相关在敏感性分析中未能保持，不能视为因果效应。

## Abstract

As the conversational AI agents become integrated into digital learning environments, students’ interaction with these systems can show conversational disengagement, including mocking, misleading, or otherwise diverting the agents. Although task-based disengagement such as gaming the system and off-task actions are well documented, the nature of the consequences of this conversational disengagement in student-AI dialogue remains largely unexplored. This study examined how different types of conversational disengagement relate to middle-school students’ mathematical learning with an AI-powered teachable agent embedded in a large-scale platform. We coded 5,522 utterances from 246 students into six conversational disengagement categories (False Answering, Mocking, Ghosting, Mirroring, Withholding, and Random/Noisy) versus engagement. Regression analyses showed that some disengagements (False Answering and Mocking) were positively associated with post-test scores after controlling for pre-test performance, although these associations did not replicate in a sensitivity analysis restricted to human-annotated utterances. To capture process-level dynamics, we modeled how conversational disengagement ends using a competing-risks approach and separately clustered students based on their turn-by-turn transition probabilities. Most conversational disengagement tended to end within the first few turns, whereas Withholding showed a strong tendency to continue. Students in the highest-performing cluster combined a moderate level of conversational disengagement with strong recovery pathways, whereas the lowest-performing cluster exhibited persistent self-loops without recovery. Together, these findings suggest that some apparent disengagement in conversation with AI may occur within interaction trajectories that subsequently return to productive engagement.

## Abstract

Goalless problems—physical scenarios presented without a specific question—have been advocated in the modelling instruction community as a tool for developing deeper engagement with physical reasoning. This paper examines the theoretical basis for this claim, drawing on cognitive load theory, mental model theory, and expert–novice research in physics, and presents a worked classroom example from an Honours Physics classroom, illustrated through a single, detailed case rather than a controlled or comparative study. Student work on an open-ended dynamics problem is analysed to illustrate how the goalless format elicits spontaneous phase decomposition, multi-representational reasoning, and self-generated physical inquiry—features characteristic of expert-like thinking that are rarely demanded by conventional problem formats. The claims made here should be read as a pedagogical argument grounded in classroom practice rather than as empirical evidence of generalisable learning gains. Practical guidance on classroom implementation and a formative assessment rubric are provided. The paper argues that goalless problems, used as a complement to conventional examination preparation, offer a principled and accessible means of developing the mental models that underlie genuine physical understanding.

> 无目标问题——只呈现物理情境而不提出具体问题——在建模教学（modelling instruction）界被倡导为一种促进学生更深入参与物理推理的工具。本文借助认知负荷理论、心智模型理论和专家-新手研究，考察这一主张的理论基础，并给出一个来自荣誉物理（Honours Physics）课堂的实例；该实例通过单个详细案例来说明，而不是对照或比较研究。文章分析了学生在一道开放式动力学问题上的作业，以说明无目标形式如何引发学生自发地把过程分阶段、使用多种表征进行推理，并自己提出物理问题——这些都是类似专家思维的特征，而常规题型很少要求学生这样做。本文的主张应被理解为基于课堂实践的教学论证，而不是可推广学习收益的实证证据。文中提供了课堂实施的实用建议和一份形成性评价量规。本文认为，无目标问题作为常规考试准备的补充，为发展支撑真正物理理解的心智模型提供了一种有原则且易于实施的途径。

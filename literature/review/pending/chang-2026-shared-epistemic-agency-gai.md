## Summary

## Key finding A

Seventh graders writing scientific explanations with a prompted generative AI showed seven kinds of shared epistemic agency, and over 80% showed it at least once. How often they did so was linked to prior language proficiency.

> 七年级学生在设定好提示的生成式 AI 帮助下写科学解释，表现出七类共享认知能动性，超过 80% 至少表现一次；表现频率与先前语言水平有关。

## Key finding B

Analysing conversation logs, assessments and self-efficacy questionnaires from 122 Taiwanese seventh graders in a bilingual photosynthesis unit, the authors identified seven types of student-AI shared epistemic agency. A four-profile cluster solution suggested agency was activity-specific.

> 作者分析 122 名台湾七年级学生在双语光合作用单元中的对话记录、测验和自我效能问卷，识别出七类学生与 AI 的共享认知能动性；四类群体的聚类结果表明能动性随活动而变。

## Limitations

Biology and English-language writing with seventh graders in one context. The findings are correlational and do not test learning gains against a control group.

> 对象为单一情境中的七年级生物与英语写作；结果为相关性，未与对照组比较学习收益。

## Abstract

Generative artificial intelligence (GAI) is increasingly used as a pedagogical assistant in online learning; yet how young students, such as secondary school students, enact epistemic agency (EA) when learning with GAI remains underexplored. In this study, we examined the types and levels of EA demonstrated by secondary school students with scaffolded GAI and investigated the relation among students’ prior academic proficiency, self-efficacy in learning, and EA demonstrated. Participants were 122 seventh graders who engaged in an online bilingual inquiry unit on photosynthesis. The unit supports students in Taiwan in learning science inquiry and English as a foreign language, with GAI prompted and integrated to support students’ scientific explanation writing in English. Data collected and analyzed included students’ prior academic proficiency through assessments, self-efficacy in learning through questionnaires, and student–GAI conversation turns through textual conversation logs. Results identified seven types of student–GAI shared epistemic agency (SEA), with over 80% of students demonstrating such agency at least once. Correlation results indicated that the frequency of EA demonstrated by students was associated with their prior language proficiency. Cluster analysis with the retained four-profile solution further suggested that student–GAI SEA was likely to be activity-specific. The identified SEA categories provide a framework for examining student–GAI SEA, and the instruction design illustrates how GAI can be prompted to preserve opportunities for student contribution. Design implications for supporting students with different needs are discussed.
